# Cue — Leads that go quiet

**5-minute pitch** · Anushree Khubele · Upivot Pro Phase B · August 2026

---

## 1. The problem

> A company has a good sales conversation, sends a price, and then the customer goes quiet. **Not a no — just silence.** Months later, nobody remembers what was discussed. So follow-up is either neglected or delayed, and the deal dies of silence rather than rejection.

This is not about finding new customers. It is about the ones already found, already spoken to, and then lost track of.

```
Good conversation        →   They were interested
        ↓
Price sent               →   Proposal goes out
        ↓
Silence                  →   Nobody said no
```

---

## 2. Why this matters

### 40–60%

of qualified B2B deals end in **"no decision"** — the customer never chose a competitor, they just never decided.

*Source: [The JOLT Effect](https://www.jolteffect.com/blog/what-is-the-jolt-effect) — Dixon & McKenna, from 2.5 million recorded sales calls*

**The cheapest pipeline there is.** A lead who already knows the company — who took the meeting, saw the price, and said they were interested — is the cheapest pipeline a firm will ever have. It is also the one nobody is working.

Cold email reply rates are now around 3.4%, down from 5.1% in 2024 — so old leads are worth more than they used to be. *([Instantly 2026 benchmark report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026))*

---

## 3. Why does it stay broken?

| # | |
|---|---|
| **1** | The customer stops replying after the proposal. |
| **2** | Attention moves to the next deal. Nobody is assigned to the quiet ones. |
| **3** | Nothing tracks it — and a small team cannot hold forty old deals in their heads. |
| **4** | The CRM stores the conversation, but it is passive. It waits to be opened. |
| **5** | Watching forty deals for something to change takes time nobody has. |
| **6** | **Every tool built for small firms solves the sending — not the watching.** |

---

## 4. The root cause

> # The reading is done.
> # The deciding is not.

AI can already summarise an old email chain. What nothing does is watch it — and tell a firm that a quiet deal has become worth reopening, when, and what to say.

---

## 5. I have already built a smaller version

At Omdena our deals sat on Monday.com, but the board only told us what stage a deal had reached — never what had actually been said.

So we connected Claude to read the emails and meeting notes and give us one line on each deal. It ran inside our weekly review, and real people used it.

|---|---|
| **22** | months doing this job |
| **12** | dead accounts recovered |

## 6. Who it's for — and what I need to test

**Who buys**

- A founder or the one person doing sales at a small B2B services firm — consultancy, dev shop, implementation partner.
- Deals in lakhs, three to nine month cycles.
- Big enough to need a CRM, too small to make one fit.

**What I still don't know**

- Will a small firm pay for this, or just agree that it's annoying?
- Is there enough left in an old email thread to tell why a deal stalled?
- Will they let a tool read their inbox?

**Next:** 8–10 interviews. First question — *"show me your list of quiet deals."* If they can't produce it, the problem isn't real for them.
