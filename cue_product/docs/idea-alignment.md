## SECTION 1 · THE OPPORTUNITY

### Problem / Opportunity Identified:

**A company has a good sales conversation, sends a price, and then the customer goes quiet. Not a no — just silence. Months later, nobody remembers what was discussed. So follow-up is either neglected or delayed, and the deal dies of silence rather than rejection.**

- This is not about finding new customers. It is about leads a company has already found, already spoken to, and then lost track of.

- A small B2B services firm might have forty of these sitting in a spreadsheet - each one a real conversation with a real, interested person. Nobody has looked at that list in months.

### Why This Could Be Valuable:

A lead who already knows the company — who took the meeting, saw the price, and said they were interested — is the cheapest pipeline a firm will ever have.

The research supports it: across 2.5 million recorded sales conversations, [40 to 60 per cent of qualified deals end in "no decision"](https://www.jolteffect.com/blog/what-is-the-jolt-effect) rather than a loss to a competitor. For a firm with deal sizes in lakhs, recovering a single deal will be a quick win.

### Why Explore Now

CRMs still can't do this. AI can read emails now — that part is solved. But nothing acts on what it read, and nothing tells you when a quiet lead is worth going back to. The reading is done. The deciding is not.

Cold outreach is getting worse every year; average cold email reply rates are now around 3.4 per cent, down from 5.1 per cent in 2024, with one agency reporting its own campaigns falling from 6.8 to 5.8 per cent in a single year. [Instantly 2026 benchmark report.](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)

So old leads are worth more than they used to be, and for the first time it is cheap enough to work them properly.

## SECTION 2 · THE 5 WHYs — ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

| Level | Question | Answer / Insight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Why 1** | Why do these companies lose deals? | Because the customer stops replying after a proposal. |
| **Why 2** | Why does the customer stop replying? | Their priorities changed, the budget moved, or the person who liked the idea left. Nobody said no. It just went cold. |
| **Why 3** | Why do priorities change without the company noticing? | There is no proper warm up, followup process. Because once the proposal goes out, attention moves to the next deal. Nobody is assigned to the quiet ones, so nothing is watching them. |
| **Why 4** | Why is there no proper follow-up process? | Lack of tracking is the primary cause. Most CRMs merely log manually entered data or retain basic meeting transcripts. Either way, it just sits there. So the work falls back on people — and in a small team, the person doing sales calls is also delivering the work. They cannot hold forty old deals in their head. |
| **Why 5** | Why doesn't the CRM handle it? | Some CRMs do store the conversation, and a summary can tell you what the customer wanted and what they were worried about. But a CRM is passive. It waits to be opened. It never tells you a quiet deal has become worth reopening. |
| **Why 6** | Why doesn't anyone check it themselves, then? | Because checking means watching forty dormant deals for something to change — a budget cycle resetting, the company hiring again, new funding. Nobody has time for that, so the follow-up goes out when someone happens to remember, which is rarely the right moment. |

### Root Cause Statement:

Every tool in this space solves the sending. The actual problem is knowing which quiet deal is worth reopening, when, and what to say.

### Where this root cause comes from:

At Omdena, our deals sat on Monday.com, but the board only told us what stage a deal had reached - never what had actually been said. So we connected Claude to read the emails and meeting notes and give us one line of the discussion.
